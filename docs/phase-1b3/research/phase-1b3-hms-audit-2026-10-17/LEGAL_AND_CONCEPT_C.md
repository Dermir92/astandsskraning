## Statutory framework and limits

| Rule and exact source | Classification | Product implication / interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L1 Arts. 1-2: scope and residential tenant protections | A, within an applicable lease | Residential rental duties do not govern unrelated vehicle reports or every standalone condition record. Conversely, labeling a lease-related inspection “standalone” does not remove parties' actual legal duties. Keep document purpose separate from an assertion of legal compliance. |
| L1 Art. 4: written lease; HMS publishes forms | A for written lease and publication duty; C for choosing that form/layout | No requirement was located compelling this product to reproduce the HMS grid, use A-G scores or use an HMS-branded document. Some other notices in the Act expressly require standardized forms; that should not be generalized to this inspection form. |
| L1 Art. 6(1), (2), (4), (13): lease identity, description, property/unit and representatives | A for lease contents | These are lease requirements. Art. 71 separately requires inspection-declaration property/lease/party identification. They do not justify collecting every party's national ID in every unrelated report. |
| L1 Art. 6(5): separate electricity/hot-water meters at handover and reading date | B: relevant separate meter exists, lease/handover context | Conditional capture is justified. An unspecified synthetic electricity value with no reading date is not sufficient to claim this part of a lease package is complete. Meter number is useful form guidance; distinguish it from the statutory reading/date requirement. |
| L1 Art. 6(10), Art. 69(1), as amended by L3 Arts. 5, 7 | A for the applicable lease cohort | Initial findings, including fire observations, must be recorded in the dedicated lease inspection section. A report reference alone is not verified as sufficient. Provide principal results suitable for that section; whether an incorporated annex satisfies it needs confirmation. |
| L1 Arts. 14 and 63 | A | Capture cleanliness, functionality, supplied fixtures and relevant deterioration at handover/return. Normal wear is legally relevant, but a user selecting “wear” is not a binding finding of immunity from liability. No automatic fault/damages determination. |
| L1 Art. 69(1) | A for inspection with lease formation and end; B if either party requests an independent examiner | Separate the ordinary parties' inspection from the agreed-examiner route. The current wording includes fire protection; older guidance says “before handover.” L3 Art. 10 requires cohort assessment. |
| L1 Art. 69(2)-(5) | B for applicable examiner circumstances; professional conduct duties | Record a reported request and agreement only if supplied. A unilateral creator-role choice does not establish agreed appointment or neutrality. Cost allocation is not a mandatory universal billing field in the app. |
| L1 Art. 70; L2 Art. 37 | Repealed | Do not implement historical Art. 70 cost/municipal-inspector rules as current requirements. Relevant cost rules are now in Art. 69. |
| L1 Art. 71 | B: examiner inspection under Art. 69(1)/(2) | Text specifies agreed examiner, landlord/tenant or representatives present, detailed condition declaration, opportunity for objections/remedies, exact premises, supplied fixtures, lease date and parties. A later online “review” does not prove attendance at the inspection. Absence/refusal handling needs advice; do not invent deemed participation. |
| L1 Art. 72 | B: at least the declaration route in Arts. 71-72; broader reach unresolved | Declaration signed by lease parties and examiner, made in three copies, each retaining one; inspection is an evidential basis in a return dispute. Exact application to party-led inspections without an examiner, electronic copies and alternative digital execution needs lawyer confirmation. A typed name or “reviewed” button does not satisfy this by itself. |
| L1 Arts. 8, 13; L4 Arts. 5, 20 | B: relevant written notice, amendment or execution | Current Art. 13 permits electronic or handwritten signing, subject to applicability. It does not establish that email OTP, a signature image or any particular vendor mechanism suffices in all circumstances. Retain this as a future execution contract, not a new fake status. |
| L1 Arts. 16, 64 and 13 | B: objection/claim, event timing and delivery conditions | App comments are not necessarily statutory notices. Art. 16 includes four-week handover objections and 14-day notice of later-discovered hidden defects; Art. 64 addresses four-week landlord claims/reservations after return or discovery. Art. 13 addresses delivery. Product receipt/approval states must not purport to override those rules. Do not build a deadline engine from the audit. |
| L1 Art. 67 | B: tenant changed locks | All keys to changed locks must be returned. A universal law requiring a key-count form was not established; a handover/access inventory is useful product practice. Never collect secret access codes just to document device transfer. |
| L1 Art. 68 | B: moving out | Tenant supplies an address for correspondence. A return-report contact field can help, but the condition-report app is not automatically a legally effective notification channel. |
| L1 Art. 5; L5 | A/B for applicable residential leases | Registering a lease is distinct from creating an inspection PDF. No HMS upload/registration status may be inferred from report generation. Keep integration out of Phase 1B.3b. |

## Official form findings

H1 §12 and H2 support room-based condition records, traceable remarks and a concise findings summary, alongside visible fire questions. The grid is optional when principal findings are recorded. Therefore Concept C's readable room-record structure can remain; a matrix-shaped report is not necessary merely to resemble HMS.

HMS's A-G legend combines age-sensitive condition descriptions with separate meanings for newly painted surfaces and cleaning. Our independent condition/function/cleanliness dimensions do not establish a valid one-to-one conversion. Preserve explicit user answers and ordinary prose. Do not label them “HMS grades” or manufacture mappings. If importing old HMS records is later authorized, retain the original literal code and legend/version separately.

H2 is a lease extract, with lease execution fields. H3 is a differently packaged older declaration with inspection-event metadata and three-copy language. Neither form alone resolves the legal interpretation questions above. A preference for an official form is not statutory certification of the data entered.

## Fire-safety scope

The obligation to consider fire protection is distinct from the app's ability to certify technical safety. R1 Arts. 5 and 7 establish substantive escape-route/equipment duties; R3 Art. 8 addresses extinguisher servicing. R1's amended detector wording is functional, and R2 §9.4.3 separately has an area/audibility benchmark. A simple count, photograph, test-button response or visible service date cannot establish whole-property compliance.

**Minimum product proposal (D, justified by A/B rules):** a short residential fire block recording detector presence/count/location and whether an ordinary test was actually performed; extinguisher presence/location and visible service-label information; and visible escape-route obstruction/not-examined status. Retain an explicit unknown/not-tested option. Optional blanket presence follows official form guidance. Do not instruct users to discharge extinguishers, open electrical systems, perform a destructive inspection or claim specialist certification.

Missing required equipment must not be recorded as “Á ekki við.” Unknown test/service status is not a positive result. An omitted fire section in a lease-related report should be conspicuous in the overview and completeness summary, even when the rest of the rooms are recorded.

## Concept C: preserve the design, add justified information later

| Area | Current design assessment | Necessary proposal and reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First-page identity | Strong address/reference, draft labels and executive overview; actual date/recorder honestly unknown | Use the existing metadata area for actual event date, recorder/capacity, purpose and, only for rental context, lease/property/party identity. Permit a continuation metadata page rather than shrinking text. Distinguish handover, lease and inspection dates. |
| Scope and limitations | Good report/room context and explicit uninspected/untested records | Preserve. Capture actual scope; do not infer a performed visual inspection or test from an item existing in a checklist. Identify omitted premises/common areas. |
| Fire protection | Material gap: no dedicated observations, only general non-certification language | Add a short distinct section or ordinary report rows using the same typography. Summarize missing fire data on the cover. This is a justified content extension, not a redesign. No layout is changed in this audit. |
| Rooms, fixtures and inventory | Complete synthetic room records and evidence structure; synthetic heating coverage exceeds current app template | Preserve. Add only justified applicable items when separately approved; include heating and supplied-inventory coverage. Do not make every possible HMS grid cell mandatory in every room. |
| Handover | Existing optional panel can accommodate keys/meters; current values are synthetic | Rental handover must distinguish conditional meter obligations from optional key logging. Show reading date and meter applicability/identity when relevant. Do not require this panel for unrelated standalone records. |
| Findings and evidence | Strong: all deviations reachable, normal wear distinguished, full prose, source/media/page references | Preserve. Retain temporary-versus-permanent evidence disclosure in the actual app adapter; synthetic packaged photos do not prove durable product media support. No assumed captured time or contributor. |
| Participation and remarks | Honest unknown participation/signature state, but lacks actor-attributed party objections | Use the existing support section for separate attendance, declared remarks, review response, execution and distribution states. A self-reported attendance name is not a verified participant submission. Allow continuation pages as needed. |
| Silence wording | App-level principle is sound; unqualified legal wording is too broad | Proposed wording: “Kerfið skráir ekki samþykki eða yfirferð án skýrrar staðfestingar. Lögbundnir tilkynningarfrestir geta samt haft áhrif á rétt aðila. Athugasemd í þessum drögum er ekki staðfest sending til gagnaðila.” Lawyer should approve final rental copy. |
| Initial/final relationship | Current approved fixture is standalone and claims no baseline | Preserve honesty. A rental final report may reference an actual baseline or explicitly state it is absent; no invented before/after conclusions or automatic damage liability. |
| Signature/certification | No fake signature or certification | Keep it that way. The design can reserve ordinary space for genuine future execution records; blank lines or a visual seal do not establish legal validity. |

The current composition has no page cap and has already accommodated long prose. Nothing in the verified sources establishes an incompatible compulsory report layout. Extra metadata/remarks may add pages; do not force everything onto the existing first page or promise the synthetic 25-page count after adding fields. New legal-content fixtures require separate A4/readability verification in implementation.

## Legal/HMS confirmations still needed

Confirm the proper form/version; Art. 72's reach for party-led versus examiner-led records; digital copies/execution; lease-section incorporation by annex; nonattendance/refusal; applicability to older renewed/extended leases; and appropriate qualifications when a specialized technical assessment is requested. Until resolved, describe the product as a draft condition-recording tool, not an HMS-approved, legally certified or professionally verified inspection.
